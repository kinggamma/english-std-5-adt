Using similes, metaphors and personifications to write a four-line stanza
(a) Read and act out the following dialogue.
Gift: Hi, Jayden.
Jayden: Hi, Gift. How’re you?
Gift: I’m fine, and you?
Jayden:
I’m fine too. Thanks.
Gift:
Sorry, my brother, I heard our friends, Fausta and Semu, talking about using similes, metaphors and personifications in sentences. Do you understand these words? How do we use them?
Jayden:
Okay, I only know how to use similes in sentences. It is when we compare two things using the words ‘as’ and ‘like’.
Gift:
Wow! Could you give examples for both words?
Jayden:
Yes, you can say, “This milk is as cold as ice.” or “This milk is cold like ice.” Here, we’ve compared milk with ice.
Gift:
Thank you. Now I can also compare two things, as you have done. For example, “The mango was as sweet as sugar.”
Jayden:
Great! But as I told you, I don’t know how to use metaphors and personifications. Let’s ask Fausta and Semu for help.
Gift:
Sure! Luckily, we see them coming here too.
Jayden:
Hello, Fausta and Semu! We’re discussing how to use similes, metaphors, and personifications in sentences. But we only know how to use similes, which involve comparing two things using ‘as’ and ‘like’. We don’t know how to use metaphors or personifications. We need your help, please.
Fausta:
Right! We use metaphors when we compare one thing or person with another with similar characteristics. Here, we do not use the words ‘as’ and ‘like’. For example, we can say, “Juma is a lion.” This may imply that Juma is as brave as a lion.
Semu:
As for personifications, we use them when we give qualities or characteristics of humans to non-living things. For example, “The sun smiled down on the village,” or “The snow whispered as it fell onto the ground.” Here, the sun and snow are given the human characteristics of smiling and whispering.
Gift and Jayden:
Aha, thanks a lot! Now we understand, and we can use them confidently.
Fausta: My absolute pleasure!
Questions
1. What words are used when forming similes, according to Jayden? Give one example from the dialogue.
2. What is the difference between a simile and a metaphor, according to Fausta?
3. What example of personification does Semu give in the dialogue, and what human quality is used?
4. Why did Gift and Jayden ask Fausta and Semu for help?
