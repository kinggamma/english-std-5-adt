“Boda boda [motorcycle] drivers played singeli on loudspeakers. Those new rhythms flooded our neighbourhoods and I couldn’t help but fall into its nets,” he [Ramadhani Mhando] says.

Muhando – known as K-Zungu – is a singeli artiste; the first the country has known with albinism. Zungu means ‘white man’ in Kiswahili, and K is the first letter of my grandfather’s name, Kaniki. That’s why I chose that name,” he says [during the interview].

Albinism is caused by the lack of pigmentation in skin, hair and eyes and affects one in 1,400 Tanzanians. This hereditary condition is bound with superstition. Some people believe that the body parts of people with albinism can bring wealth or cure illnesses.

The myth has led to attacks and killings of people with albinism in the country. UN statistics show that 75 people with albinism were murdered in Tanzania between 2000 and 2016.
