Grandmother’s pumpkin

Once upon a time, in a small village, there lived a wise old grandmother, called Mama Siti. She loved to plant pumpkins in her garden. One year, she had grown the biggest pumpkin she had ever seen. It was bright orange and sat proudly in the middle of her garden, just waiting to be harvested.

One morning, a curious hare hopped by. The hare, known for high speed, would often admire Mama Siti’s garden. Today, his eyes landed on the giant pumpkin. “Oh, what a beautiful pumpkin!” He said to himself. “I will nibble on it when Mama Siti is not looking.”

The hare waited patiently for Mama Siti to leave the garden. As soon as she was out of sight, the hare hopped to the pumpkin and took a big bite. But to his surprise, the pumpkin skin was too tough to bite. “This is not tasty at all!” he exclaimed.

Mama Siti, who had been watching the hare from behind a tree, smiled and called out, “Little hare, the pumpkin was meant for sharing, not stealing. If you had asked, I would have given you a piece.”

The hare felt ashamed and embarrassed. “I’m sorry, Mama Siti. I should have asked.”

From that day on, the hare learned an important lesson: It’s better to ask for what you want than to steal.
